# Formal Methods (Core Course)

## Description

An introduction to the formal, statistical, and mathematical tools that
show up increasingly in contemporary philosophy of science, aimed at
building enough literacy to critically engage with research that uses
them. Covers formal logic, probability theory, Bayesian approaches to
scientific reasoning, and the basics of decision and game theory — and how
each shapes current debates in philosophy of science.

## Learning outcomes

By the end of the course, students should:

1. Be familiar with some of the core formal methods used in philosophy of
   science.
2. Have a basic understanding of how those methods are used in the field.
3. Be able to apply basic formal techniques to analyze and solve selected
   problems in philosophy of science.

## Format

Weekly Friday lectures (Apr 17 – Jul 17), plus a weekly Monday tutorial
where a tutor reviews the previous lecture and addresses exercise
questions.

## Assessment

- **Studienleistung:** five exercise sheets released Fridays, two weeks to
  complete each (strict submission deadline, no late work without prior
  arrangement). Max. 50 points per sheet (250 total). Credit requires (a)
  at least 125 points overall **and** (b) at least 63 of those points from
  sheets 3–5 — both conditions are independently required.
- **Prüfungsleistung (module exam), choice of:**
  - **Term paper**, due Sep 1 (firm deadline barring documented prolonged
    illness) — topic must be agreed with the instructor in office hours,
    registered by Jul 1.
  - **Oral exam** (30 min, mid-July to mid-September) — register by Jul 1
    with three possible topics; covers roughly 3–4 lecture sessions.
  - Either way, the topic must come from the syllabus, and students must
    also meet the Prüfungsamt's own registration deadlines.

Collaboration on exercises is allowed but must be disclosed (list
collaborators on the sheet); final write-ups must be independent, and
using AI tools to generate exercise solutions counts as academic
misconduct.
