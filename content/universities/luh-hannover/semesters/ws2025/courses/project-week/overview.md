# Project Seminar / Project Week

## Description

A two-part module. The **Project Seminar** (weekly, Oct–Jan) builds core
research, writing, and argumentation skills through short facilitated
sessions and small weekly assignments, culminating in a mini-essay. The
**Project Week** (Feb–Mar) is where students apply those skills to their
own independent research project, working toward a term paper and a short
research presentation.

Officially the two can be taken separately (Project Seminar is a
Studienleistung, Project Week is the graded Prüfungsleistung/module exam),
but the syllabus strongly discourages splitting them — budget time after
the semester ends to prepare for Project Week, and after Project Week to
revise the final paper.

## Learning outcomes

By the end of the module, students should:

1. Understand what constitutes a philosophical question and be able to ask
   one.
2. Know the basic structure of philosophical texts and be able to identify
   theses and arguments in them.
3. Be able to examine and evaluate philosophical arguments (validity and
   soundness; argument/fallacy types).
4. Be able to work independently on a philosophical question — literature
   search, generating and situating questions, using examples, constructing
   arguments.
5. Know the formal requirements of academic philosophical work (citation,
   bibliography, avoiding plagiarism).

## Format

### Project Seminar (Thursdays 16:00–18:00, Oct–Jan)

Weekly sessions moving through three blocks:

- **Block I — Reading and doing philosophy:** how to read philosophical
  texts, identify theses and arguments, and start constructing your own.
- **Block II — Researching and writing a mini-essay:** starting a project,
  structuring a paper, using philosophical tools (examples, test cases,
  counterexamples), culminating in a mini-essay due Jan 8.
- **Block III — Getting started on your own research project:** reflecting
  on the mini-essay process, referencing/citation practice, and developing
  the research question that carries into Project Week.

See `sessions/` for the week-by-week topics, pre-readings, and exercises.

### Project Week (Feb 1 – Mar 30)

A two-month follow-up during which students complete an independent
research project: a 5,000-word paper plus a 15-minute presentation. Fixed,
non-negotiable milestones:

| Date | Milestone |
| --- | --- |
| Feb 1 | Start the research project (writing included) |
| Feb 23–27 | Project Week: give the 15-min presentation, receive feedback |
| Mar 8 | Submit first full draft of the paper |
| Mar 15 | Receive instructor feedback |
| Mar 15–30 | Revise |
| Mar 30, 23:59 | Submit final paper |

## Assessment

- **Studienleistung (Project Seminar):** complete all weekly assignments,
  do the readings, and attend regularly.
- **Prüfungsleistung (Project Week):** attend the in-person project week
  and submit the final paper.
