# Chapter 4 — Theory, Measurement, and Absolute Temperature

- **Narrative:** The quest for the theoretical meaning of temperature.
- **Analysis:** Operationalization — making contact between thinking and
  doing.

## Discussion questions

- Why did William Thomson (Lord Kelvin) conclude that it's unsatisfactory
  to define temperature relative to a particular substance (e.g. air)?
- How did Thomson arrive at his preferred definition of "absolute
  temperature," and what does it entail epistemically (p. 202 ff.)?
- Did the Joule–Thomson experiment let them correct air thermometers so
  that air thermometers could measure "absolute temperature"?
- What are Thomson's three theoretical definitions of temperature grounded
  in, respectively — and why does the chapter treat the differences between
  them as irrelevant to his overall concept of temperature?
