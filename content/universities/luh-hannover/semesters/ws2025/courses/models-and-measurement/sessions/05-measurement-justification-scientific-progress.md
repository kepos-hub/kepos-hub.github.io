# Chapter 5 — Measurement, Justification, and Scientific Progress

## Discussion questions

- What's Chang's term for his own philosophical position on measurement and
  scientific progress?
- What does that position entail, what view of science does it rely on, and
  how does Chang defend it?
- On this picture, how can "scientific progress be measured"?
- Does Chang's explication of quantitative measurement hold together across
  the book? Earlier he distinguishes thermoscopes from thermometers by
  whether they're "graduated by [...] principles that would give systematic
  meaning to their readings" (p. 41) — i.e. numbers whose arithmetical
  operations yield meaningful results. On p. 229, he distinguishes ordinal
  from cardinal *quantities* instead, which seems to sit awkwardly with
  reserving "thermometer" for instruments with a quantitative scale
  determined by an identifiable method (p. 41).
