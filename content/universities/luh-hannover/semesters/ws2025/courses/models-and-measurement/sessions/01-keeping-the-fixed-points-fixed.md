# Chapter 1 — Keeping the Fixed Points Fixed

- **Narrative:** What to do when water refuses to boil at the boiling
  point.
- **Analysis:** The meaning and achievement of fixity.

## Discussion questions

- How were the fixed points (of the boiling and freezing of water)
  established? What do "superheating"/"supercooling" experiments show about
  the challenges of fixing those points on the eventual temperature scale?
- Does Chang succeed in his explication of quantitative measurement (think:
  ordinal vs. cardinal scale)? Chang follows Middleton's distinction between
  *thermoscopes* and *thermometers* (p. 41): one is "graduated by [...]
  principles that would give systematic meaning to their readings," the
  other is not.
- How does Chang try to justify that numerical thermometers "allowed a true
  quantification of temperature" (p. 48)? Does the attempt succeed?
